# Storyline: Q3 2026 board update (Sample data)

## 0. Brief

- Purpose: Q3 board update for a fictional B2B SaaS company ("Sample Co.") that gets one decision from the board
- Audience: Board of directors (3 investors, 1 independent); they know the $12.0M year-end ARR plan and last quarter's deck
- Decision: Should the board approve converting two Q4 sales hires into onboarding specialists, and accept a revised year-end ARR forecast?
- Deck type: board update
- Presenter: CEO, Sample Co.
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Language: en
- Length: 12 main slides + 2 appendix
- Data status: sample (every number is invented for testing and labelled "Sample data")
- Assumptions: Q4 new ARR, expansion and contraction repeat Q3; SMB churned ARR rises by the same amount as Q2→Q3 (see E12); new AEs need ~5 months to ramp (notes.md)

## 1. Governing message

> Q3 beat plan, but rising SMB churn now puts year-end ARR about $230k short; moving two planned sales hires to onboarding cuts that gap by about a third and fixes the cause.

## 2. SCQA

- Situation: The board approved a plan of $12.0M ARR by year end, and new bookings have beaten plan every quarter this year.
- Complication: SMB churn more than doubled since Q1; if the trend continues, year-end ARR lands about $230k below plan.
- Question: Will we hit plan, and what should the board approve now?
- Answer: Not fully; approve moving two Q4 sales hires to onboarding, which recovers about a third of the gap this year and removes the cause for 2027.
- Order on slides: answer-first

## 3. Key arguments

- Split: results / constraint / cause / fix (board-update pattern: what happened, what limits us, why, what we do)
- Order: logic chain

1. New business is healthy: Q3 new ARR beat plan by 21%, the third straight quarter above plan — answers: "Is this a demand problem?"
2. SMB churn is now the binding constraint and puts year-end ARR about $230k short — answers: "Why change anything if we beat plan?"
3. The churn comes from SMB accounts that never finish setup, as onboarding load rose 41% — answers: "Is it product, price or onboarding?"
4. Moving two Q4 sales hires to onboarding attacks the cause and saves $110k a year — answers: "What does it cost us, and what do we give up?"

MECE check:
- Overlap: 1 (new business) and 2 (retention) split ARR movement into inflow vs outflow; 3 explains 2 without restating it (cause vs size); 4 is the action and its cost.
- Gaps: hardest objection is "fewer AEs hurts 2027 growth" — covered on slide 11 but not yet sized: [DATA NEEDED] kept open. Expansion and mid-market are stable and stay in the appendix.

What changed between this storyline and `deck.json`, and why. No number was added that is not in the evidence ledger or `inputs/`.
