# Storyline: Which Southeast Asian market first?

## 0. Brief

- Purpose: Recommend which Southeast Asian market our subscription language-learning app should launch first
- Audience: CEO and CFO; they know the product and the one-team launch budget, not the markets
- Decision: Which market first: Indonesia, Vietnam, the Philippines or Thailand?
- Deck type: market entry
- Presenter: Head of Growth (hypothetical company)
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Language: en
- Length: 11 main slides + 2 appendix
- Data status: real (sourced) public statistics; the company is hypothetical
- Assumptions: (1) internet users are the reachable audience for a mobile app; (2) GDP per capita (PPP) is a usable proxy for ability to pay when comparing countries; (3) no internal test data exists yet

## 1. Governing message

> Launch in Indonesia first: it has 2.4 times Vietnam's online audience at similar income per person, but a six-week paid test should confirm we can win before the full budget is committed.

## 2. SCQA

- Situation: The app is profitable at home and we can fund one new-market launch team in the next 12 months.
- Complication: The four candidate markets differ widely in online audience, income and growth, and we can only afford to launch one.
- Question: Which market should we launch first?
- Answer: Indonesia, the largest online market even after adjusting for income, confirmed by a six-week paid test against Vietnam before full spend.
- Order on slides: answer-first

## 3. Key arguments

- Split: size of the reachable market / its value after income / the best alternative / our ability to win and the cost to find out
- Order: importance

1. Indonesia is the largest reachable market: 206M internet users, 2.4 times Vietnam's — answers: "Where are the users?"
2. Adjusted for income, Indonesia's online market is at least twice that of any other candidate — answers: "Big, but can they pay?"
3. Vietnam is the best backup: the fastest-growing economy, but under half Indonesia's online audience — answers: "Why not the faster-growing market?"
4. Whether we can win is unproven in every market, so a six-week paid test should gate the full launch — answers: "What could make this wrong, and how do we find out cheaply?"

MECE check:
- Overlap: 1 is headcount only; 2 adds income only; 3 compares the runner-up on growth; 4 is everything public statistics cannot show (spend, competition, acquisition cost, rules).
- Gaps: hardest objection is "Indonesians may not pay for a language app". Public statistics cannot answer it, so argument 4 turns it into a test and the gaps are listed as data requests. Working-age share and mobile subscriptions were checked and do not separate the four markets (appendix).
