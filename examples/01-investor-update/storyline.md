# Storyline: Duolingo FY2025 investor update

## 0. Brief

- Purpose: Brief investors on Duolingo's FY2025 results and explain what the 2026 plan means
- Audience: Investors who follow the company and know the headline numbers but have not read the filings in detail
- Decision: How should investors read the sharp slowdown in 2026 guidance, and what should they track to judge it?
- Deck type: investor update
- Presenter: SoWhat Decks example
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Language: en
- Length: 12 main slides + 1 appendix
- Data status: real (sourced) — FY2025 10-K, Q4 FY2025 and Q4 FY2024 shareholder letters
- Assumptions: 2026 quarterly results are out of scope (the brief limits material to the annual report and letters); not investment advice, no buy/sell view

## 1. Governing message

> After a profitable $1B year, Duolingo is deliberately trading 2026 bookings growth and margin for faster user growth, so investors should judge 2026 by DAU growth.

## 2. SCQA

- Situation: Investors backed Duolingo to keep growing users and turn that growth into profit; in 2025 it passed $1B in revenue with wider margins.
- Complication: User growth slowed sharply in 2025, and 2026 guidance cuts bookings growth to 10-12% and the adjusted EBITDA margin to 25%.
- Question: Is the 2026 slowdown a warning sign or a choice, and how should we judge it?
- Answer: It is a stated choice to give up near-term bookings and margin for user growth, funded by strong cash flow, so judge 2026 by whether DAU growth re-accelerates.
- Order on slides: answer-first

## 3. Key arguments

- Split: what happened (results) / why it matters (user engine) / what management is doing (2026 plan) / whether it can afford it (cash)
- Order: logic chain

1. 2025 delivered scale and profit: revenue rose 39% to $1.04B and adjusted EBITDA margin reached 29.5% — answers: "Is the business healthy?"
2. User growth slowed: DAU growth fell from 51% to 30% and paid subscriber growth from 43% to 28% — answers: "Why change course after a record year?"
3. The 2026 plan gives up about 5 points of bookings growth and accepts lower margins to grow users faster — answers: "Is the slowdown demand or choice?"
4. $360M of free cash flow and $1.04B in cash can fund that bet and a $400M buyback — answers: "Can they afford it?"

MECE check:
- Overlap: 1 covers financial outcomes only; 2 covers user metrics only; 3 covers forward guidance only; 4 covers cash and capital only. Net income quality sits in 1, not 4.
- Gaps: hardest objection is "the slowdown is weak demand dressed up as strategy". Arguments 2 and 3 show the timing, but the annual material cannot prove the bet is working; that is the open data request on slide 12.
